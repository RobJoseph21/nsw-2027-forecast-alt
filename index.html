Methodology
1.Smooth recent NSW polls (LOESS through the Wikipedia poll table) to estimate today's vote share per party.
2.Use 2023 NSW primary votes as the per-seat baseline; Pittwater (2024) and Kiama (2025) by-elections override.
3.Pool IND and OTH together throughout — polling can't separate them and sitting Independents would otherwise get crushed.
4.For One Nation, replace each seat's input with its 2023 NSW upper-house (LC) vote share, proportionally trimming the other parties so totals stay at 100%.
5.Per seat, run Proportional Swing (scale 2023 by per-party poll-shift ratios) and Transition Matrix (redistribute via a 5×5 voter-flow matrix from the YouGov federal poll, 28 Apr – 5 May 2026, scaled to NSW polling). Calibrate each to poll state totals.
6.Hybrid: 50:50 PS+TM for ALP, COA, GRN, IND, OTH. For One Nation, blend PS and TM by University-degree rank — high-uni seats lean PS, low-uni seats lean TM. Re-calibrate all six parties to poll state means.
7.2CP: top three parties by primary, use real 2023 ballot data (4.7M ballots) to eliminate the third, then apply seat-specific preference flows to compute winner and margin. Fall back to NSW-wide flows where seat data is missing.
