Rob Joseph 7/5/26
Methodology (1 of 2)
1.Scrape NSW Legislative Assembly poll table from Wikipedia.
2.Fit a smooth curve (LOESS) through the polls to estimate today's NSW vote share per party.
3.Use 2023 NSW general-election primary votes as the per-seat baseline.
4.Override Pittwater and Kiama baselines with their 2024/2025 by-election results.
5.ON didn't run in 76 seats in 2023; estimate what they would have got there using their Legislative Council vote × the average LA/LC ratio in seats where they did run.
6.Pool IND and OTH into a single bloc throughout — polling can't tell them apart and sitting Independents would otherwise get crushed.
7.Proportional Swing forecast: scale each seat's 2023 primary by (poll share ÷ 2023 share) per party.
8.Transition Matrix forecast: redistribute each seat's 2023 primary using a 5×5 voter-flow matrix derived from the YouGov federal poll (28 Apr – 5 May 2026), scaled to NSW polling.
Rob Joseph 7/5/26
Methodology (2 of 2)
9.Calibrate both forecasts so each method's seat-weighted state totals match the polls (ALP, COA, GRN, ON, and combined IND+OTH).
10.Hybrid forecast: the average of the calibrated PS and TM.
11.For each method, take the top three parties by primary as the candidates likely to survive eliminations.
12.Use real 2023 ballot data (4.7 million ballots, full preference orderings) to determine which of those three drops out third, leaving the actual final two.
13.Apply seat-specific 2-party preference flows (also from 2023 ballot data) to compute the 2CP winner and margin between the remaining two.
14.Where the seat's own data isn't available (e.g. ON didn't run there), fall back to a NSW-wide average of those flows.
15.Output: one row per seat with the 2023 baseline, the three forecasts, the three 2CP winners and margins, and a few diagnostic flags.
